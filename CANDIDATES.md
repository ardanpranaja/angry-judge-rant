# Rant candidates — "Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest" (u-riidzhsn-angry-judge-the-short-rant-contest-b9f46046)

Judge brief, in order of weight: (1) a funny rant about an everyday
annoyance, not a general essay or a list of complaints; (2) actually funny —
a real laugh, not a smirk at how hard it is trying; (3) a specific, concrete
annoyance, not a vague mood; (4) sharp, economical wording, every sentence
earning its place; (5) freshness — an angle the judge has not heard a
hundred times.

Gate: /output/entry.txt must be UTF-8 text of 1–400 characters, not only
whitespace. All candidates below were counted and are inside the gate.

## Scoring notes

- **#1 — winner.** A rant, not an essay (criterion 1); the laugh is the
  absurd contradiction — a black-and-white document held hostage by a
  colour nobody asked for (criterion 2); the printer, the deadline and the
  cyan cartridge are concrete, not a mood (criterion 3); the staccato
  "Cyan. The document is black and white." is three short beats, no
  throat-clearing (criterion 4); the printer-running-out-of-ink trope is old,
  but the black-and-white-document angle is the fresh turn on it
  (criterion 5). The anger is shared — everyone has been held up by a
  printer at exactly the wrong minute — rather than performed.
- **#4 — runner-up.** Very concrete (the bagging-area voice, the rice) and
  the "smuggling it" payoff is a real laugh, but "unexpected item in the
  bagging area" is one of the most-quoted annoyances in existence, so it
  loses on freshness.
- **#12 — third.** Sharp and specific ("per my last email"), and the triple
  "I know / I watched / now we are both here" builds nicely, but the joke
  is about a phrase, not an event, so it lands less viscerally than #1.
- **#6 — fourth.** "It knows" is a strong final beat and the timing
  observation is relatable, but Wi-Fi dying mid-call is a well-worn rant
  with no new angle.
- **#9 — fifth.** "Mating" in an email to the boss is a genuine laugh, but
  the autocorrect joke is a known shape and the red-underline beat is
  decoration, not payoff.
- Cut for being a vague mood rather than a concrete event: #3 (Monday
  morning dread is the brief's own example of a tired topic), #11 (the bus
  is concrete but "personal vendetta" is a mood, not a punch).
- Cut for weak or decorative payoffs: #2 (the laptop sweating is a nice
  image, the rant itself is thin), #5 (funny setup, but "thanking a
  spreadsheet" is the only laugh), #7 (the second beep is a real
  annoyance, but the rant states it rather than landing it), #8 (the
  jet-bridge line is good, the setup is long for the payoff), #10 (the
  list escalates well, but "or hope" is the only joke and 170 characters
  is a lot to spend on it).
